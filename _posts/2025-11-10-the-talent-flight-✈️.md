“I’ve decided,” said Akash, scrolling through his phone. “I’m applying for jobs in Canada. My course fits well there, and the pay is decent.”

“Even I’m thinking the same,” Jiya added. “My brother’s already there. He said he can help me settle until I find something stable.”

Kabir frowned slightly. “So both of you are planning to leave? Why not try here first?”

Akash leaned back. “Because here, everything moves slow. Fewer openings, lower pay, and no real recognition. What’s the point of struggling when you can grow faster abroad?”

Jiya nodded. “It’s not about running away, Kabir. It’s about moving where opportunities exist.”

This is not only the story of Akash and Jiya but of millions who leave their home country and settle abroad for higher education, better opportunities, and recognition. In fact, India is among the world’s largest sources of emigrants, with millions moving abroad every year in search of better prospects. But this is not just about money or personal growth; it’s about the nation losing its skilled students and professionals like doctors, engineers, scientists, and innovators.

*As per December 2021 data*

Every year, more than 2.5 million people emigrate from India for work or study. This is where the idea of brain drain comes in. ***Brain drain refers to the large-scale movement of educated and talented individuals from their home country to another for better opportunities, higher salaries, or improved living conditions.***

The United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) views brain drain as a serious loss of human capital. It estimates that India loses around $2 billion due to the migration of computer professionals to the United States alone. Overall, the economic loss is estimated between $35 to $50 billion every year in forgone growth, especially in sectors like healthcare, technology, and research.

*It’s like planting trees here but watching them bear fruit in another land. We celebrate when a student joins NASA but forget that ISRO also needs such talent.*

The main reasons behind brain drain are lack of opportunities, low salaries, poor working conditions, limited freedom, and an uneven quality of life. The losses are not just economic but also emotional and social. The country loses the potential of its brightest minds who could have contributed to innovation and progress at home.

However,there is another side too. *Some who leave return with experience, ideas, and investments. They bring back a global perspective that can help improve systems here. This is known as **Brain Gain.***

India is now the world’s largest recipient of remittances, receiving about 120 billion dollars in 2024, which supports families, reduces poverty, and strengthens the economy. Prime Minister Modi once called brain drain a **“brain deposit,”** *saying that knowledge gained abroad can eventually help India.* Dr. Jaishankar also believes that the *world today is like a “membrane” where people can move in and out easily. India, he says, has become a magnet for opportunities, and even Silicon Valley now looks toward the “valleys of India.”*

India has already started moving in the right direction. With growing startups, global companies investing here, and the rise of young innovators, the country is slowly turning into a land of new possibilities. But to truly stop the talent flight, there’s still a need to make this growth more inclusive, where opportunities reach not just cities, but every deserving individual across the nation. To reduce brain drain, the government should create more jobs, fair pay, and better opportunities so people don’t have to look abroad for growth. Support for startups, innovation, and research should increase, and education must be strengthened with more scholarships and better facilities. Improving daily life from transport to healthcare and safety can also make people feel proud to stay. But citizens have a role too. Supporting local businesses, encouraging young talent, and holding leaders accountable can make real change. If we all believe in our country’s potential, this talent flight can turn into a story of progress.

In the end, chasing dreams abroad isn’t wrong, but true success is when those dreams also help build the nation that raised us.
