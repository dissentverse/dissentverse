Generation Z, often shortened to Gen Z and also known as Zoomers, are those who are born between 1997 to 2012. This is the generation which actually grew up with internet, where memes are their language and social media is their hub. Still, most of the time we hear people saying that this generation is irresponsible, careless, always stuck on phones and yes, sometimes even depressed. But one thing is 100% true – their opinions, views and mindset are totally different from the last generation.

They don’t just want to study, get a degree, and do a job. They want a lot more. They want to explore new things, question this society, and see the world in their own way. And being a Gen Z myself, I can totally feel it. I’ve even studied a bit on this topic, but today I’m not here to talk about Gen Z in general. I’m here to talk about Nepal, the protest that is being led by the students and the youth , The Gen Z.

***✊ Gen Z in Nepal’s Protest***

Gen Z is often called careless and irresponsible, but when it comes to their nation, they become unstoppable. The live example is Nepal, where a national protest started by the youth and students shook the whole system. They came out against corruption, tax evasion, nepotism and malpractices that were eating the country from inside.

**T*o understand this properly, let’s go a little back in history.***

Earlier, Nepal was an autocratic monarchy. Kings ruled, citizens had no right to speak or express their opinions, and they had to blindly follow whatever the government ordered. People were living under control, while those in power were enjoying all benefits. Then came the long Nepalese Civil War (1996–2006), after which the monarchy system ended. In 2008, Nepal was declared a secular and democratic republic. Sounds great, right? But the truth is – it was not truly democratic.

Yes, elections happened every five years, but only two parties The Nepali Congress and the Communist Party of Nepal kept ruling, sometimes in coalition with smaller ones. Before elections, they made promises. After winning, they forgot everything. Result? The country remained in miserable conditions , poverty, unemployment, lack of education, and almost zero development.

What triggered the youth the most was when they saw the children of politicians living in luxury, showing off expensive cars, parties, and their lavish lifestyle on social media, while ordinary people struggled even for basics. Youth realized where their tax money was going. So, they started raising voices on social media, making content, criticizing leaders, and spreading awareness. But instead of listening, the government tried to shut them down, government banned 26 social media platforms, claiming they weren’t registered.

Now just think , in today’s world, where most businesses like hotels, tourism, online shopping, freelancers, and even small shops depend on the internet, this ban was not just about social media fun, it was directly about people’s livelihood. And this became the final spark that lit the fire.

**🔥 From Peaceful Protest to Violence**

![](https://dissentverse.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/img-20250917-wa0003.jpg?w=690)

At first, the protests were peaceful. Students gathered, chanted slogans, used posters and online campaigns. But when the government couldn’t control them, they ordered the forces to stop them. When that also failed, police and army started shooting at protesters.

Around 72 people lost their lives, including *a school student who was just standing in support of his nation.A college student was shot near his eye and chest, but after getting treatment, he still came back to protest again.*

This brutality made the anger of youth uncontrollable. They stormed into the Parliament (Singha Durbar), broke barricades, and even set parts of it on fire. Not only that, protesters also attacked houses of politicians. The saddest incident happened when they set fire to the house of former PM Jhala Nath Khanal. His wife, Rajyalaxmi Chitrakar, who had no fault, died in that fire.

All of this finally shook the Prime Minister K.P. Sharma Oli. Scared and under huge pressure, he resigned from his post. Currently, Nepal has an interim PM, but the situation is still tense.

This is not just about one country. Corruption, unemployment, tax evasion, and nepotism these problems are everywhere. But when the system ignores the citizens for too long, protests like this are bound to happen.

Every government should learn from Nepal. If you don’t listen to the citizens, if you don’t fulfill your promises, and if you keep eating up people’s money, one day the youth will rise. And when Gen Z rises, they don’t stop.

***⚖️ The Two Sides of Gen Z***

Of course, this is not the full picture. On one side, we see Gen Z students risking their lives, standing strong for their country. On the other side, there are still youth wasting their time, stuck in meaningless online fights, arguing over religion and identity instead of their nation’s future.

So yes, Gen Z is not always right. They are not perfect. But one thing is very clear that when they stand for their nation, they are powerful enough to bring down even the strongest governments.

*Nepal’s Gen Z has shown the world that change is possible. Their courage is proof that youth may be called careless, but they are the ones who can create revolutions. Maybe it’s time every country realizes that the power of the future lies with the youth, and ignoring them can be the biggest mistake.*

This brings me to a question – if something like this ever happens in India, will our Gen Z rise and stand together for the nation like Nepal’s youth did? Or will we remain distracted in things that don’t really matter? It’s something we all need to reflect on.
